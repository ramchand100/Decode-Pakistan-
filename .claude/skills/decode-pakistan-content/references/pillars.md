# Content Pillars

Every episode should sit mainly in one pillar. The best episodes also touch a second
pillar — that overlap is usually where the "why this works" angle comes from.

## 1. Business case studies

A specific Pakistani company, brand, or business figure — rise, fall, reinvention, or a
single high-stakes decision. Think School's bread and butter, translated: this works best
when the company is one most viewers recognize (a bank, a textile group, a telecom, a
consumer brand) but the *mechanism* of what happened is not widely understood.

Good angle shapes:
- A company everyone knows failed, but not why, specifically
- A decision that looked smart at the time and wasn't (or vice versa)
- A founder/family story where the business is really a proxy for a bigger structural issue
  (succession, regulation, access to capital)

## 2. Economic explainers

A mechanism in the Pakistani economy, explained through what it actually does to people and
businesses, not abstractly. IMF programs, circular debt, currency devaluation, the tax base,
remittances, energy pricing, inflation's second-order effects.

Good angle shapes:
- "Everyone talks about X, almost no one can explain how it actually works" (circular debt,
  the IMF's conditions, why the rupee moves the way it does)
- A number that sounds abstract made concrete (what a 10% devaluation actually does to a
  specific business's costs)
- A policy's *unintended* effect, not just its stated purpose

## 3. Geopolitics

Pakistan's position between China, the US, Gulf states, India, and Afghanistan — where a
relationship, deal, or event has consequences that aren't obvious from the headline.

Good angle shapes:
- A deal (CPEC, IMF, a bilateral agreement) explained through what each side actually gets,
  not the press-release version
- A historical alignment decision whose consequences are still playing out today
- "Why does Pakistan keep doing X" — explained through incentives, not narrative

## 4. Policy/history

A specific law, institution, or historical decision, and how it shaped the country's present —
told as a chain of cause and effect, not a timeline.

Good angle shapes:
- An institution whose original purpose is very different from what it does today, and why
- A historical decision (partition-era, a specific military or civilian government's policy)
  whose effects are still visible in a very concrete, present-day way
- "The rule nobody remembers voting for" — a policy whose origin is obscure but whose effects
  are everywhere

## Worked examples (illustrative — verify before using)

- *Business*: "How Engro went from a single fertilizer plant to a conglomerate spanning food,
  energy, and telecom — and the 2008 bet that nearly broke it." (Business, touches Economic)
- *Economic*: "Why Pakistan's circular debt keeps coming back no matter which government
  promises to fix it — traced through what actually happens between a power plant and a
  consumer's bill." (Economic, touches Policy)
- *Geopolitics*: "What China actually gets out of Gwadar that isn't in the official CPEC
  narrative." (Geopolitics, touches Economic)
- *Policy/history*: "The one-unit policy's dissolved decades ago — so why does its shadow still
  show up in how provinces fight over the NFC award today?" (Policy, touches Geopolitics/Economic)

These are illustrative shapes, not verified scripts — always research and confirm facts,
figures, and framing before turning one of these into a real topic row or script.
