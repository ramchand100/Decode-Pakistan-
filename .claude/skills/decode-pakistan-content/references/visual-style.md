# Visual Style Notes

These aren't final production rules — they're patterns worth defaulting to when writing a
script's beat headings, so an editor has cutaway/graphic cues to work from even before
Remotion or a human editor gets involved. Mark these in the script as a bracketed note after
the relevant line, e.g. `[cut to: mechanism diagram]`.

## Format mix

Alternate between the presenter on camera and full-graphic segments rather than staying in one
mode the whole video. A natural default: presenter delivers the hook and payoff (the two
moments that benefit most from a human face and direct address), while explainer/mechanism
beats go full-graphic so data and diagrams have room to breathe.

## Citing a source on screen

When a claim comes from a specific article, report, or dataset, show it — a screenshot of the
actual source with the publication name visible, with the one sentence that matters highlighted
or pulled out rather than making the viewer read the whole thing. This does double duty: it
backs up the `[Source, date]` citation from the script, and it signals credibility visually
without the narration having to say "according to."

## Mechanism diagrams

For a multi-step mechanism (money or goods moving between parties), use simple icon
figures with names and round numbers, stepped and numbered (1, 2, 3) rather than shown all at
once — this mirrors the "chain-of-mechanism escalation" writing technique in
`script-template.md`, so the visual beat and the script beat should be built together. Any
invented/illustrative numbers on screen should carry a small, consistent disclaimer note (e.g.
"for illustration only") so they're never mistaken for a real cited figure.

## B-roll as metaphor

Stock footage works best as a visual stand-in for an abstract idea (a commodity, a trade route,
a price moving) rather than literal documentation of the story. Cut it fast — a couple of
seconds is usually enough to land the metaphor before returning to the presenter or a diagram.

## Segment-coded palette

Using a distinct, consistent background treatment per segment *type* (not per episode) — e.g.
one look for mechanism diagrams, another for the presenter's desk setup, another for the outro
— helps the viewer register "we've switched modes" instantly, which matters more in a
fast-cut explainer than a matching brand palette across the whole video.
