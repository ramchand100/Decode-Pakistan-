# Hook Patterns

A hook is the first 5-15 seconds — it has to earn the rest of the video. Write hooks exactly
as spoken, not as a description of what the hook will do. For each topic, produce one hook per
pattern below so the choice is between genuinely different angles.

## 1. Question hook

Poses the question the whole video answers — but the question itself has to be one the viewer
didn't know they had, not one they'd guess the answer to instantly.

> "Pakistan's biggest bank almost collapsed in 2008 — and almost nobody outside the industry
> knows why. What actually happened?"

Weak version (avoid): "Have you ever wondered how banks work in Pakistan?" — too generic, no
stakes.

## 2. Shocking-stat hook

Opens on a number that's surprising *once you understand what it implies*, not just a big
number. The stat should do work — set up the tension the video resolves.

> "In 2022, Pakistan spent more paying interest on its debt than it collected in income tax
> from the entire country. That's not a crisis — that's the system working exactly as
> designed. Here's why."

The stat must be real and sourced before it goes in a script — flag `[NEEDS SOURCE]` at the
topic stage if unverified, and never let an unverified number reach a finished script.

## 3. Contrarian-claim hook

States a claim that goes against the popular narrative, then promises to justify it. This
works especially well for geopolitics and policy topics where there's a widely-repeated
"villain" explanation.

> "Everyone blames the IMF for Pakistan's economic pain. The IMF isn't the reason Pakistan
> keeps ending up back at their door — this is."

Use this pattern carefully: the video must actually deliver a defensible case, not just
manufacture controversy. If the evidence doesn't clearly support the contrarian framing, don't
force it — use a different hook pattern instead.

## 4. Story cold-open hook

Drops the viewer into a specific moment — a decision, a meeting, a single day — before zooming
out to the bigger story. Works well for business case studies with a clear inciting event.

> "On a Tuesday morning in October 2008, the founder of one of Pakistan's largest textile
> groups signed a single contract that would wipe out three family fortunes within eighteen
> months. This is how it happened — and what it reveals about the industry nobody saw coming."

## Choosing between them

- Business case study with a clear inciting decision → story cold-open usually wins
- Economic mechanism most people misunderstand → question or shocking-stat
- Widely-repeated but shaky popular narrative → contrarian-claim
- When unsure, generate all four and let the user pick — different hooks suit different
  audiences and thumbnail/title pairings, so this is a real creative decision, not just a
  formality.
